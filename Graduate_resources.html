Graduate Student Resources

Graduate school can be an exciting opportunity to pursue scientific questions, develop new skills, and become part of a research community. It can also be difficult to navigate, especially when you’re figuring out how to apply, find funding, or identify opportunities for training and professional development.

I’ve collected some resources that may be helpful for prospective and current graduate students, particularly those interested in oceanography, marine biology, microbial ecology, and environmental science.

These lists are not exhaustive, and funding opportunities, eligibility requirements, and application deadlines can change. Always consult the official program websites for the most current information.

Applying to Graduate School

  • UC Santa Barbara Interdepartmental Graduate Program in Marine Science admissions guide — An excellent guide to graduate school admissions, with advice applicable well beyond UCSB.

  • The Hidden Curriculum of Graduate School — A resource shared by the University of Wisconsin–Madison about navigating expectations, challenges, and aspects of graduate education that aren’t always explicitly explained.

  • Example application materials — A collection of example statements for graduate school applications, NSF Graduate Research Fellowship applications, postdoctoral positions, and faculty positions.

When exploring graduate programs, I encourage prospective students to consider not only the scientific questions they want to investigate, but also the mentoring environment, funding arrangements, opportunities for professional development, and experiences of current students.

Graduate Fellowships and Research Funding

Funding can come from a variety of sources, including university assistantships, external fellowships, research grants, and scholarships. Some opportunities support students before they begin graduate school, while others are intended for students already enrolled in a program.

Funding directories

  • Student Funding and Research Opportunities Spreadsheet — A community-compiled directory of funding, research, travel, outreach, and training opportunities for undergraduate students, graduate students, and postdoctoral researchers. The spreadsheet includes separate tabs and filters to help identify relevant opportunities. Last updated October 2024, so some deadlines, eligibility requirements, and links may be outdated.

Federal and national fellowship programs

  • NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP) — Graduate research fellowships supporting eligible students pursuing research-based graduate degrees.

  • NASA Graduate Student opportunities — Funding and research opportunities across NASA science disciplines, including Earth and space science.

  • American Meteorological Society Graduate Fellowships — Graduate fellowship opportunities in atmospheric, oceanic, and related sciences.

  • NOAA Dr. Nancy Foster Scholarship Program — Support for eligible graduate students pursuing research relevant to NOAA’s mission.

  • Link Foundation Ocean Engineering & Instrumentation Fellowship — Fellowship opportunities for graduate research in ocean engineering and instrumentation.

  • American Association of University Women Fellowships and Grants — Funding opportunities supporting graduate education, research, and professional development.

Other graduate funding and support

  • NSF NCAR’s Advanced Study Program’s Graduate Visitor Program (GVP) Fellowship — Opportunities for graduate students to conduct research with scientists at NCAR.

Graduate Positions and Scientific Careers

Finding graduate positions and research opportunities often requires exploring several sources, including professional organizations, departmental websites, mailing lists, and community-maintained directories.

  • Graduate Positions in Oceanography and Marine Science — A shared spreadsheet of graduate research opportunities.

  • Ocean Carbon and Biogeochemistry (OCB) Jobs and Postdocs — Research positions and career opportunities in ocean biogeochemistry.

  • ECO Magazine Career Opportunities — Employment and research opportunities across ocean science and technology.

Community-maintained lists can be especially helpful, but may not always be up to date. Confirm openings and application instructions directly with the institution or research group.

Training and Professional Development

Graduate education extends well beyond a dissertation project. Specialized courses, research cruises, workshops, internships, and professional networks can help you develop skills, explore new approaches, and connect with researchers outside your home institution.

Research cruises and ocean observing

  • UNOLS Cruise Opportunities — A starting point for learning about opportunities to participate in research cruises and connect with the oceanographic research fleet.

  • UNOLS Chief Scientist Training Cruise Program — Training opportunities focused on cruise planning, shipboard operations, and scientific leadership.

  • NSF Education and Training Application (ETAP) — A searchable platform for NSF-supported research and training opportunities.

Ocean optics and remote sensing

  • NASA/University of Maine Calibration and Validation for Ocean Color Remote Sensing Workshop — Specialized training in ocean color remote sensing, field measurements, and satellite validation.

  • Cornell Satellite Remote Sensing Summer Course — Training in satellite oceanography and remote sensing applications.

Marine microbiology and ocean science

  • Marine Biological Laboratory Microbial Diversity Course — Advanced training in microbial diversity, ecology, and laboratory approaches.

  • Friday Harbor Laboratories Summer Courses — Field- and laboratory-based courses in marine science.

Internships, policy, and science communication

  • William M. Lapenta NOAA Student Internship Program — Opportunities for research and professional development in NOAA-related disciplines.

  • MBARI Summer Internships — Research and engineering internships in ocean science and technology.

  • Sea Grant Knauss Fellowship — A fellowship introducing eligible graduate students to ocean, coastal, and Great Lakes policy in Washington, D.C.

  • ASLO Science Communication Internship — Professional development in communicating aquatic science.

  • ASLO Multicultural Program — Opportunities for eligible students to participate in scientific meetings and build professional networks.

Scientific Communities and Support Networks

Scientific communities and professional networks can provide mentorship, collaboration, friendship, and a sense of belonging beyond your home laboratory or institution.

The following organizations and networks support scientists across different disciplines, backgrounds, identities, and career stages.
